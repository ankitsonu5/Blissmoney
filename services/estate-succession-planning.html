Give your family greater clarity about the wealth you leave.

Wealth passes between people as well as generations. A thoughtful succession plan brings together your intentions, asset ownership, family responsibilities and the documents that support them. BlissMoney helps organise the financial picture and the conversations that need to happen, while qualified legal and tax professionals determine appropriate structures and documentation. The objective is a clearer path for your family, with fewer decisions left unexplained.

Some important decisions should not be left implicit

Families often understand what they own better than how it would be administered or transferred. Planning helps clarify intentions while the relevant people can participate and important information remains accessible.

Assets are held across people and entities

Personal accounts, jointly held property, business interests and existing legal arrangements may have different implications. A coordinated inventory helps identify where ownership, documentation and intended succession need specialist review.

Family responsibilities are changing

Marriage, separation, bereavement, new dependants or changing care needs can make earlier arrangements unsuitable. Revisiting the plan helps ensure that the right questions reach the appropriate legal and tax professionals.

The next generation needs context

A list of assets does not explain the decisions behind them. Structured family conversations can establish responsibilities, explain intentions and help beneficiaries prepare for the practical demands of future stewardship.

Connect intentions information and professional execution

Estate information and ownership map

Organise the assets, liabilities, ownership records and existing arrangements within the agreed scope. Record where nominations, beneficiary details or supporting documents need to be checked by the relevant institution or legal professional.

Family priorities and continuity needs

Discuss who may need financial support, who is expected to make decisions and which responsibilities require continuity. Identify practical questions around access to information, ongoing expenses and the administration of family affairs.

Coordination with legal and tax specialists

Prepare a clear brief for professional advice on wills, trusts or other appropriate arrangements. Track the decisions and documentation involved, while legal drafting, structural advice and tax opinions remain with qualified specialists.

Family communication and review

Help organise conversations about intentions and responsibilities at a level you are comfortable sharing. Establish triggers for review, including material asset changes, family events, residency changes and changes advised by your specialists.

From the question to an agreed plan

Phase 01

Understand intentions and sensitivities

Start with your priorities, family context and existing arrangements. Agree who should participate and how sensitive information will be handled during the engagement.

Phase 02

Organise the estate picture

Create a working inventory and identify information gaps. Separate financial coordination tasks from legal questions that require a professional opinion.

Phase 03

Coordinate the required decisions

Work from the specialist brief to organise meetings, outstanding questions and agreed actions. You retain control of decisions and the appointment of professional providers.

Phase 04

Keep the arrangements current

Review the financial picture and action status when circumstances change. Confirm with the relevant specialists whether existing documents or structures need updating.

Clarity before commitment

Q. Is estate planning only relevant for very large estates?

The need depends on your responsibilities, not only asset values. Dependants, jointly held assets, business ownership and assets in different locations can create questions worth addressing with professional support.

Q. Will BlissMoney draft my will or establish a trust?

Legal drafting and advice on structures must be provided by qualified legal professionals. The proposed service focuses on financial organisation, preparing the brief and coordinating agreed actions with the professionals you appoint.

Q. Do nominations and a will need to be reviewed together?

They should be considered as part of a coordinated review. Their effect can depend on the asset, governing rules and individual circumstances. A qualified legal professional should explain how the relevant arrangements operate in your situation.

Q. Can planning begin before the whole family participates?

Yes. An initial review can clarify your intentions and existing information. Whether and how other family members participate is a separate decision, informed by your preferences and professional advice.

Q. What changes should prompt a review?

Material changes in family circumstances, ownership, liabilities or residency can be useful triggers. A significant acquisition or sale may also affect earlier intentions. Your legal and tax professionals should advise on changes affecting the documents or structures.
